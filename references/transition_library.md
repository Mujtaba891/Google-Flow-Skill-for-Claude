# Transition Library

Transitions carry as much "designed vs. generated" signal as the shots themselves. Never default to a hard cut when a motivated transition is available — but also don't use a flashy transition where a simple cut would read as more premium.

## Types

- **Swipe**: a full-frame directional wipe between scenes, matched to the exit direction of the previous scene's motion (if the last element exited left, swipe left). Fast (0.2-0.3s).
- **Morph**: one shape/element becomes the anchor of the next scene (e.g. a card corner morphs into the next scene's background gradient). Requires a genuine visual link between the two scenes — don't force it.
- **Zoom (match-cut push)**: push into a detail of scene A until it fills frame, cut to scene B already zoomed to a matching scale, then pull back. Excellent for "diving into" a feature.
- **Whip**: fast directional blur-pan, camera whips past at high speed, next scene resolves out of the blur. High-energy only — reserve for promo-burst style.
- **Spin**: element or camera rotates through the transition, common for 3D/isometric object reveals (e.g. rotating a phone from app A's screen to app B's).
- **Stretch**: an element elastically stretches to fill frame becoming the next scene's background — playful, use sparingly.
- **Liquid**: organic blob-morph transition, associated with premium/abstract brand content — glowing orbs merging/splitting into the next scene.
- **Blur transition**: simplest premium option — foreground defocuses, scene cuts, new scene resolves into focus. Reliable default when nothing more specific is motivated.
- **Camera match cut**: camera motion continues seamlessly across the cut (e.g. push-in ends scene A, push-in continues into scene B) — the single best "invisible" transition for maintaining momentum, works especially well with Flow's scene-extension feature.
- **Shape transition**: a recurring brand shape (circle, rounded-square) wipes or scales through frame as the connective device between all scenes in a sequence — gives a multi-scene explainer a consistent signature.

## Selection guide

Use a **match cut / camera match** by default for continuous narrative flow. Use a **swipe or shape transition** when scenes are more modular/independent (feature list format). Reserve **whip, spin, stretch** for high-energy promo content only.
